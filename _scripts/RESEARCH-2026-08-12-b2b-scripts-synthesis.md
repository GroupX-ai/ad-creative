# B2B SaaS ad-script research synthesis (2026-08-12)

Produced by a 7-agent research sweep: web research on direct-response script frameworks,
hooks, UGC/founder formats and CTA/offer framing, plus a live Meta Ads Library pull of
~2,000 ads from big B2B SaaS advertisers (ClickUp, monday.com, Grammarly, Notion, Rippling,
Motion) and ~250 ads from direct competitors (Instantly, Smartlead, lemlist, Apollo,
Earlybird, ScaledMail, Prospeo, Drop Cowboy, Slybroadcast, ZeroBounce, NeverBounce).
Ad longevity (days active) is used as the public proxy for conversion; vendor claims are
labelled unverified. Feeds batch 5 (`seedance-prompts-b5.mjs`).

## FORMULA: The Meta B2B stack: UGC pain hook > raw screen recording > one hard number > free close
WHY: Strongest evidence in the corpus: convergent across ~2,000 live ads in six B2B SaaS Meta libraries (verified via Ads Library API 2026-08-12). Screen recording is the #1 video format for ClickUp (21% of library), monday.com (27%), Grammarly (26%), Notion. Every scaled advertiser leads with exactly one defendable number (ClickUp 'Forrester verified: 384% ROI' and '$19,000/year saved', VoiceDrop's own '9%+ Callback Rate', Notion 'Up to $12,000 free Notion credit'). No scaled advertiser runs brand-feel video without a direct-response close. Convergent evolution under spend is the best public proxy short of private conversion data.
BEATS 15s: 0-2s: pain or status hook to camera, no logo, caption mirrors spoken line. 2-9s: raw screen recording of ONE workflow end to end (drops sending, invalids flashing red). 9-12s: the one number on screen ('9%+ callback rate'). 12-15s: end card with numbered free offer + arrow to the Meta button.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: pain/status UGC hook ('My boss thinks I cold-called 500 people today. I never dialed once.'). 3-6s: one line of context, product still unnamed. 6-18s: screen recording of the single workflow, cut fast, real UI. 18-25s: the one number as text + voice. 25-30s: free CTA said aloud, shown on end card, matching the button text.
EXAMPLE: VoiceDrop 30s: 'My boss thinks I cold-called 500 people today. I never dialed once.' > screen capture: upload list, hit Send, 500 voicemails drop, callback log fills in real time > 'Teams on VoiceDrop see a 9%+ callback rate.' > 'Your first drops are free. Tap Sign Up below.' (ClickUp's verbatim source hook: 'My CEO thinks I'm an Expert Project Manager but I literally just type one sentence...')
BEST FOR: Default template for all three. Strongest for VoiceDrop and 1Lookup, where the product action is visually dramatic (drops going out, invalid numbers flashing red live).

## FORMULA: Compressed PAS, hardened with the Harmon qualify + mechanism beats
WHY: Highest-spend evidence for the open: Grammarly's top YouTube ad (est. $53M+ spend per VidTao) opens with pure problem audio ('Writing's not that easy...') before any branding; monday.com ran 'Sick of switching between dozens of browser tabs...?' for years at large spend; Slack's founder-targeted ad holds the product back until 24s. The two added beats come from Harmon Brothers (Squatty Potty: ~140M views, $100M+ sales in 2016): bluntly qualify the viewer by role, then explain the mechanism before the demo. A Motion-cited B2B test found pain-first angles beat feature-led by 180% (secondhand, unverified). Benly's '+18-28% conversion' for PAS is a vendor claim, unverified.
BEATS 15s: 0-3s: pain in second person ('You called 100 prospects today. 3 picked up.'). 3-6s: mechanism in one line ('Nobody answers unknown numbers anymore.'). 6-12s: solution resolving that exact pain on screen. 12-15s: CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: pain in second person, no logo. 3-6s: qualify by role ('You know who hates cold calling? Your reps. And everyone they call.'). 6-12s: mechanism, why the old way is structurally broken. 12-22s: product demo fixing exactly that. 22-27s: one proof number. 27-30s: CTA with the friction removed.
EXAMPLE: VoiceDrop: 'You called 100 prospects today. Three picked up. It's not your reps, nobody answers unknown numbers anymore. VoiceDrop leaves a voicemail without their phone ever ringing, and prospects call YOU back. 9%+ callback rate. Start free below.'
BEST FOR: Cold traffic for all three. Best where the mechanism is the wedge: VoiceDrop (no ring, they call back) and Emailchaser (why emails land in Promotions).

## FORMULA: ROI-math native ad (the Motion 542-day pattern)
WHY: Single strongest longevity datapoint in the corpus: Motion ran one UGC variation for 542 days closing on 'Try it free for 14 days!'; ClickUp's free-forever closer ran 1,042 days (adkit.so days-active data; advertisers kill losers fast, so days-active is the best public conversion proxy). The pattern: a text-message-style hook that blends into organic Reels, then ROI math the viewer computes themselves (30 min invested > 11h saved = 22x). Verbatim Motion hook: 'Them: what's your secret? Me: I spend 30 minutes on Monday to save 11h/week.'
BEATS 15s: 0-3s: text-exchange hook on screen. 3-8s: the math stated flat ('15 minutes of VoiceDrop = a full day of dials'). 8-12s: one product proof flash (screen recording). 12-15s: free-trial CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: text-message hook, native look, zero branding. 3-10s: day-in-life or desk footage that reads as organic content. 10-20s: the ROI math built on screen so the viewer does it in their head. 20-26s: quick screen-recording proof. 26-30s: numbered free CTA.
EXAMPLE: VoiceDrop: on-screen iMessage bubble: 'Them: how are you booking 20 meetings a week without cold calling? Me: 15 minutes of VoiceDrop = a full day of dials.' Cut to sending 500 drops while getting coffee, callback log filling. 'Do the math. First drops free.'
BEST FOR: VoiceDrop and Emailchaser aimed at founders/agency owners scrolling Reels; the format is built to not look like an ad, so it suits cold prospecting.

## FORMULA: Split-screen proof / Before-After-Bridge
WHY: Contrast is one of Motion's 5 validated hook tactics from the biggest dataset found (550K+ ads, ~$1.3B Meta spend, brands at $1M+/month). The receipt-as-first-frame hook is a named category across multiple sources. Doubly valuable here because the competitor sweep verified that across ~250 active cold-email competitor ads, not one claims landing in Gmail's primary tab: a proven form aimed at an unclaimed angle. Benly's '+22-32% CTR' for BAB is a vendor claim, unverified.
BEATS 15s: 0-2s: split-screen before/after as the literal first frame + one line ('Same email. One landed. One died.'). 2-8s: bridge, the mechanism doing it on screen. 8-12s: the after number. 12-15s: CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: split-screen proof, tight close-up, no logo. 3-10s: before agitation (email in Promotions, $0, unopened). 10-20s: bridge, 5-8s screen capture of the feature producing the after. 20-26s: after state with the number (14 replies). 26-30s: CTA framed as starting your own switch.
EXAMPLE: Emailchaser: frame 1 is the same email side by side in Promotions and Primary. 'This email made $0 in Promotions. Same email, primary inbox: 14 replies.' Bridge: deliverability feature on screen. Close: 'Still landing in Promotions? Start free, hit the primary inbox this week.'
BEST FOR: Emailchaser above all (one screenshot proves the entire pitch). 1Lookup variant: before = bounce report at 11%, after = under 1%, bridge = the API verifying live.

## FORMULA: Founder 'So I built it'
WHY: Practitioner convergence, no controlled study, so ranked below the library-verified formats: Reloop lists founder story as one of 3 templates that perform best for software; Sovran calls 'so I built it' one of the most powerful phrases in founder-led marketing (implies frustration, conviction, and domain expertise at once); Dara Denney reports the text Founder's Letter format 'shows incredible results' and that founder content fatigues slower than polished brand ads; Superhuman is the canonical B2B example. Low production is a feature: Gerhardt's raw 7-second iPhone clip out-performed studio content.
BEATS 15s: 0-3s: origin pain with a number ('My reps were dialing 400 numbers a day and reaching almost nobody.'). 3-6s: pivot line 'So I built [product].' 6-11s: what it does in one sentence + demo cut. 11-15s: plain CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: selfie shot, real desk, 'I run 3 sales-led companies. My reps were dialing 400 numbers a day and reaching almost nobody.' 3-12s: the one specific broken thing (specificity beats polish). 12-15s: 'So I built VoiceDrop.' 15-24s: one sentence of what it does + quick screen-recording cut. 24-30s: one proof point (users, callbacks booked) + 'Try it free.'
EXAMPLE: 'I run 3 sales-led companies. My reps were dialing 400 numbers a day and reaching almost nobody. Nobody answers unknown numbers anymore. So I built VoiceDrop: it drops a voicemail without the phone ever ringing, and prospects call US back. Teams on it book callbacks daily. Try it free at voicedrop.ai.'
BEST FOR: All three: one selfie founder ad per product, phone front camera, one take, stumbles left in. Robby is the credible peer for the founder/operator ICP.

## FORMULA: Us-vs-Them comparison / conquest, one dimension, one number
WHY: Live-library evidence: Smartlead re-cut its flat-price-vs-per-seat comparison across three separate launch waves (Apr 22, May 8, Jun 3, 2026), and advertisers only re-cut angles that produced; Earlybird's 'Instantly Alternative! pay once' ads (Aug 12, 2026) prove competitor-name conquest clears Meta review in this niche. Verbatim pattern line from the script libraries: 'Manual video production: 2 weeks. Our AI: 2 minutes.' Rule from the teardowns: one narrow dimension beats a multi-criteria comparison. SaaS Hero's claim that conquest audiences convert 3-5x is an agency claim, unverified.
BEATS 15s: 0-3s: name the old way or competitor frustration. 3-9s: split-screen contrast on ONE number. 9-12s: risk reversal or review count. 12-15s: CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: the frustration hook ('Cold calls: 3% pickup.'). 3-12s: side-by-side contrast on one concrete dimension with real numbers. 12-20s: 2-3 checkmark advantages vs pain points. 20-26s: risk reversal ('under 1% bounce or money back' style) or proof count. 26-30s: CTA.
EXAMPLE: VoiceDrop: 'Cold calls: 3% pickup. Ringless voicemail: they call YOU back.' Emailchaser conquest: 'Instantly alternative, built to land in the primary inbox, not Promotions.' 1Lookup vs Prospeo: '99.5% accuracy or you don't pay, and we validate phones in real time, not just emails.'
BEST FOR: Retargeting and category-aware audiences only, not cold prospecting. Emailchaser (deliverability conquest is unclaimed) and 1Lookup (out-sharpen Prospeo's 'Under 1% Bounce or Money Back' lead horse).

## FORMULA: Result-first testimonial (compressed Star-Story-Solution)
WHY: Same beat order appears independently in Reloop, GetHookd, and AdStellar: specific result first, skepticism voiced second, switch story, one number, casual CTA. Decades of direct-response lineage (Vince James, Brunson's DotCom Secrets). Library corroboration: Smartlead's 'Power Users. Real Results.' montage is the only customer-proof video among active cold-email advertisers and was re-launched the day after first cut, an early-winner signal. Reloop's '40% better than polished brand commercials' is a vendor claim, unverified. Ranked last because it needs real customer numbers to be honest.
BEATS 15s: 0-3s: the result ('We booked 22 callbacks last week without a single cold call.'). 3-6s: skepticism ('I thought ringless voicemail was spammy.'). 6-11s: product UI flash. 11-15s: number + CTA.
BEATS 30s: 0-3s: lead with the specific result, not the product. 3-8s: voice the viewer's own skepticism. 8-18s: short switch story with screen-capture overlay at the 'let me show you' moment. 18-25s: the before/after number. 25-30s: casual recommendation CTA ('Seriously, just try it, it's free').
EXAMPLE: Emailchaser 25s (from corpus, verbatim draft): 'Our reply rate tripled and I almost didn't sign up. I assumed every cold email tool says the same thing: primary inbox, blah blah. Then I checked where OUR emails were landing: Promotions tab, all of them. Switched to Emailchaser, same list, same copy, landed primary. Reply rate went from 1% to 3%. There's a free trial.'
BEST FOR: All three once defendable customer numbers exist. Write it backwards: pick the number first, open the ad with it. Cast presenters who ARE the buyer's peer (agency owner, SDR manager), professional setting, not a bedroom.

## HOOKS
- Direct callout / ICP qualify (published verbatim: 'If your Shopify store gets traffic but no sales, keep watching'): VoiceDrop: 'If your reps leave 50 voicemails a day and get zero callbacks, watch this.' Emailchaser: 'If you're an agency sending cold email for clients, check your Promotions tab right now.' 1Lookup: 'If a third of your lead list is dead numbers, this is for you.'
- Negative hook with blame-shift (published verbatim: 'Your demos are not converting and it has nothing to do with your product'): VoiceDrop: 'Your reps aren't bad at follow-up. Nobody answers unknown numbers anymore.' Emailchaser: 'Your cold emails aren't getting ignored. They're never being seen.'
- Myth-flip (live ScaledMail ad, Aug 2026, verbatim: 'Cold email isn't dead. You're just doing it wrong.'): VoiceDrop port: 'Cold calling isn't dead. Ringing their phone is.'
- Stop-doing-X contrarian (published template: 'If You're Still Using [Tool], You're Already Behind'): VoiceDrop: 'Stop cold calling. Your prospects' phones aren't ringing anyway.' 1Lookup: 'Stop buying bigger lead lists. Verify the one you have.' Resolve the replacement by second 5-8 or it reads as clickbait.
- Number/stat hook, only numbers you can defend: VoiceDrop: 'Sales reps leave 70 voicemails to get 1 callback.' 1Lookup: '1 in 4 numbers on a purchased lead list is disconnected.' Stat first, implication in the same breath.
- Bold claim + proof as one 3-second unit (Motion-validated tactic from the $1.3B dataset; 'a bold claim without quick evidence reads as clickbait'): 'This got 34 prospects to call US back yesterday' spoken over the live VoiceDrop callback log, never over b-roll.
- Confession (Motion-validated tactic): Emailchaser: 'I sent 10,000 cold emails to spam before I figured this out.' 1Lookup: 'I spent $4K on leads with dead phone numbers before I found this.'
- Misconception 'I thought X was just Y' (Grammarly's long-running archetype: 'I thought Grammarly was just spellcheck'): VoiceDrop: 'I thought ringless voicemail was spam. Then 9% of prospects called me back.'
- Status-flex with a twist (ClickUp's live UGC hook: 'My CEO thinks I'm an Expert Project Manager but I literally just type one sentence...'): VoiceDrop: 'My boss thinks I cold-called 500 people today. I never dialed once.'
- 'Still doing X?' question (monday.com's years-long opener: 'Sick of switching between dozens of browser tabs...?'): VoiceDrop: 'Still making 200 dials a day to book 2 meetings?' Emailchaser: 'Still sending cold email from your main domain?' 1Lookup: 'Still paying your SDRs to dial disconnected numbers?'
- Receipt-as-first-frame / screenshot hook (contrast validated in Motion's dataset): Emailchaser frame 1 = same email in Promotions vs Primary, VO: 'Same email. One landed. One died.'
- Threshold self-diagnosis (live Consulti AI ad delivering 3+ weeks: 'Bounce Rate Over 2%? Your List Is Dirty'): 1Lookup: 'If more than 2 in 100 emails bounce, Gmail flags you as spam.' Viewer qualifies themselves in second one.
- Text-message hook + ROI math (Motion's 542-day ad, verbatim: 'Them: what's your secret? Me: I spend 30 minutes on Monday to save 11h/week.'): VoiceDrop: 'Them: how do you book 20 meetings a week without cold calling? Me: 15 minutes of VoiceDrop = a full day of dials.'
- Authority/credential + number in the first two seconds (Opascope archetype): 'I run outbound for a 40-rep sales team, and this cut our cost per booked call in half.'
- Diegetic pattern interrupt (B2B version of 'stop scrolling'): open mid-action on a locked phone lighting up with an inbound call: 'Why is my phone ringing off the hook?' Then reveal it's prospects calling back. Beats stunt interrupts, which get attention but not clicks.
## CTAS
- Numbered free offer with friction removed, never a demo: 'Get 100 free lookups. No card. Tap Sign Up.' / 'Your first voicemail drops are free. Start below.' Evidence: 'Book a Demo' buttons convert ~1.5%; the longest-running SaaS ads all close free (ClickUp 'Free forever. No credit card.' at 1,042 days, Motion 'Try it free for 14 days!' at 542 days). 1Lookup already sells 100 free credits on-site, so the ad must say the number.
- Three-layer synced close in the last 3-5 seconds: voiceover speaks the exact CTA, on-screen text spells the same words, Meta button matches word for word. Pick the button for the conversion event: AdEspresso's test had 'Learn More' win clicks (+22.5% CTR) but 'Sign Up' win conversions (+14.5% CVR). Run 'Sign Up' or 'Get Offer' since the money event is card-saved trials.
- Future-benefit dated close, [verb] + now + [outcome] + [when]: 'Sign up now and your first voicemail drop goes out in 10 minutes.' / 'Verified numbers before your next send.' / 'Primary inbox by Friday.' (Published verbatim model: 'Tap now and start falling asleep faster tonight.')
- Cost-of-inaction urgency, never countdown timers: '~80% of cold calls hit voicemail anyway. Every day you dial is a day of free callbacks you didn't get. Start free.' Backed by HBR-published research (Dixon/McKenna): 40-60% of B2B deals die to indecision, and loss framing beats gain framing. Fake scarcity reads false for SaaS.
- Regret-question close (published model: 'Still wasting time on manual reporting? Book your demo today.'): 'Still dialing numbers that never ring back? Start dropping voicemails free today.' / 'Still landing in Promotions? Start free, hit the primary inbox this week.'
- Double exposure: tease the offer at ~15s of a 30s cut ('and yes, your first 100 lookups are free, more on that in a sec'), then restate in full on the end card ('Like I said: 100 free lookups, no card. Tap Sign Up.'). Two chances to act per view.
- Dollar-denominated offer as the headline for cold traffic (Rippling runs ~74 new creatives/week on 'See Rippling. Get a $150 gift card.'; Notion escalates to '$12,000 free credit'): VoiceDrop: '$20 in free voicemail drops.' 1Lookup: 'Validate 1,000 contacts free.' A concrete dollar number outpulls a feature claim.
- Point at the button, never 'link in bio': VO 'Tap Sign Up below and grab your 100 free lookups' + downward arrow animation at the Meta button. Paid placements have a native button; 'link in bio' adds two steps and is organic-Reels language. End card every time: logo + URL + one number + one bold CTA.
## COMPETITOR GAPS
- 'Primary inbox, not Promotions' is completely unclaimed: verified against ~250 active cold-email competitor ads (Apollo, Smartlead, lemlist, Earlybird, ScaledMail, agencies). Closest is Smartlead's generic 'Protect Your Inbox Reputation.' Emailchaser's exact pitch has zero Meta competition.
- Ringless voicemail is an empty auction: Slybroadcast and Drop Cowboy have zero findable Meta ads; only ~63 US ads match 'ringless voicemail' and they are mostly VoiceDrop's own plus tiny Drop Dialer. Nobody has made the category-education video: a locked phone receiving a voicemail notification with zero ring, then the callback.
- No one runs a real-time validation demo: ZeroBounce, NeverBounce, and Clearout are dark on Meta; Prospeo and the micro-players run static guarantee headlines. A live screen recording of a list being cleaned or a phone number verified against the carrier has no competition.
- Mechanism language is open while 'verified' gets crowded: ContactOut and Smartlead both now say 'triple-verified,' so it is becoming category wallpaper. 'We ping the carrier in real time' escalates to the mechanism no one claims.
- Deliverability-grounds conquest is unclaimed: Earlybird attacks Instantly only on pricing (lifetime deal vs subscription). Nobody runs 'Instantly alternative, built to land in the primary inbox.'
- Vertical UGC video itself is the gap: almost everything running in these niches (Prospeo's 185 ads, Smartlead, the validation micro-players) is static-headline creative. A genuine 15-30s UGC video with a screen-recording body enters a format vacuum, not just an angle vacuum.
## DONTS
- No logo, brand name, or 'At VoiceDrop we...' before second 5. Unanimous across 5+ independent sources; branding in the opening frames signals 'ad' at the exact moment you need engagement. (The '8-15 point hook rate lift from cutting the logo' figure is unverified, but the direction is consensus.)
- No slow fades, black-screen intros, static opening frames, or 3-second explanation buildups. The first line must be fully spoken and on screen by second 2: Meta's own research says average attention per mobile content is 1.7 seconds, and 65% of 3-second viewers reach 10 seconds, so the hook is most of the ad.
- Never make a bold claim without proof on screen within ~3 seconds. Claim and receipt (callback log, inbox screenshot, API response) are one unit; separated, the claim reads as clickbait and tanks watch-through.
- Never close these three products on 'book a demo.' Demo buttons convert ~1.5% while every long-running low-ACV SaaS ad closes free. Demo CTAs only make sense at enterprise ACV, or bribed Rippling-style.
- No naked CTA verbs ('Learn More,' 'Get Started,' 'Check Us Out') and no 'link in bio' in paid scripts. Bind the verb to a numbered offer and an immediate outcome, and point at the native button.
- No bare talking heads and no polished brand-film look. Opascope's portfolio data: storytelling-visual hooks beat plain talking-head UGC 86 to 14; polish pattern-matches to skippable advertising (Barry Hott, $600M+ managed spend). Always cut screen capture over the face at the 6-10s mark.
- Don't bait-and-switch: the hook's promise must pay off by second 6, or hook rate looks fine while hold rate and CVR collapse. Curiosity resolved too late buys clicks from people who were never prospects.
- No fake scarcity or countdown timers for B2B buyers; it reads false and burns trust. Urgency must come from quantified cost of inaction or a same-day payoff.
- Don't run one hook until it dies: Motion's data has winning hooks fatiguing ~37% after 7 days. Keep one body per product and rotate 5-10 hook variants weekly; ship each concept as 6-10 simultaneous variants like ClickUp and let the auction allocate.
- Don't put unverifiable or blended numbers in the ad: one defendable number per script (callback rate, primary-inbox rate, validation accuracy), placed right before the CTA. Inflated stats kill trust with professional audiences, and per Robby's reporting rules only the money event counts.
- Don't judge creative early or on hook rate alone: wait for ~5K impressions, kill under 18% hook rate on cold Meta traffic, pass at 25%+, and read it next to hold rate and CPA. Expect roughly 1 winner per 15-20 creatives, so cheap volume beats perfect singles.
