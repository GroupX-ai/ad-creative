# ESA Card heart-tug batch, for approval before anything is rendered

Nothing here has been generated. Standing rule: **never render a video ad without sending the
script first.**

Built to the register Robby picked (d1-viewing, d5-hotel: authentic, wholesome, the animal is
the star) and the batch-2 formula: a real errand with a transaction in it, a location change
with an arrival, a very cute animal in every frame, a wordless final beat. Every candidate went
through a three-lens panel (heart-tug, claim safety, render safety) before making this doc;
the panel killed five candidates outright, mostly for colliding with ads already live, and
every surviving script below carries its fixes already applied.

Two constraints from earlier batches, held everywhere:

- **The owner says the brand phrase, never a second character** (second characters garbled it
  into "ham card" and "elbow card" at $6.94 a roll).
- **The card is never the answer to a permission question.** The yes, the keys, or the signed
  paperwork always comes BEFORE the card appears; the card is photo identification or filed
  paperwork, never the thing a gatekeeper rules on. Three drafts died on this and were
  rewritten; the live d/e set already follows it.

New this batch, from the panel: the card prop line ("a plain white card with a small photo of
the animal and three grey lines") is written into every transaction beat, because a script
that never describes the card gets an invented printed card from the model.

## Script 11 — "Old Bones"
**Animal:** senior yellow labrador, grey muzzle. The batch's tear-jerker: nobody has run a
senior-dog ad in this category.

**0.0–3.5** The open tail of a small moving van. The old lab watches from the pavement, tail
in a slow wag.
> HER: "Fourteen years old. Stairs are done, so we found a garden flat."

**3.5–8.0** The leasing agent waits at the gate and holds out the keys first.
> AGENT: "You must be Murphy. We've been expecting you."
> HER: "And here's his emotional support animal card, for your file."

She holds out a plain white card with a small photo of the dog and three grey lines; the agent
tucks it into a folder and smiles at the dog.

**8.0–11.5** Through the empty flat to an open garden door.
> HER: "Go see."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. Murphy breaks into a short, creaky trot across the grass, the
first run in a long time, then turns and looks back at her in the doorway. She stays there
watching.

*32 spoken words. The keys come before the card on purpose; the welcome is for the dog, not a
verdict on the card. The payoff was rewritten off the e2-keys settle-into-the-sun-spot beat,
which e2 owns: this one closes on joyful movement instead.*

## Script 12 — "Visiting Nana"
**Animal:** miniature dachshund in a little harness. The panel's strongest candidate: setting
and payoff are new against the whole live set.

**0.0–3.5** At the open car boot she clips a lead onto the dachshund.
> HER: "Sunday. You know where we're going."

**3.5–8.0** She stops at the building's plain front desk (no sign-in book, no readable
paper anywhere).
> DESK: "Here for Mrs. Adler? And who's this?"
> HER: "This is him. Here's his emotional support animal card."

She holds out a plain white card with a small photo of the dog and three grey lines.
> DESK: "She's been talking about him all week."

**8.0–11.5** In the corridor the dog pulls toward one particular door. It opens and an older
woman lights up.
> HER: "Go on then, find her."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. The dog crosses to the seated grandmother and rests his chin on
her knee. Her hand settles on his head.

*34 spoken words. The desk line is an identity question, not a permission question, on
purpose: an earlier draft had "Is he registered to visit with us?", which implies the card
grants building access, and it did not survive the claim panel. The grandmother reveal was
moved out of the final beat so the close is one simple physical action.*

## Script 13 — "Night Shift"
**Animal:** staffy mix. The panel's best hook.

**0.0–3.5** Dawn light at a front door, her in work scrubs, the dog leaning on her leg.
> HER: "Twelve hours. He hates it more than I do."

**3.5–8.0** The dog walker arrives with a lanyard of keys.
> WALKER: "First morning. Anything I should know?"
> HER: "Here's his emotional support animal card. He naps at nine."

She holds out a plain white card with a small photo of the dog and three grey lines.
> WALKER: "We will be fine, won't we."

**8.0–11.5** One shot: the dog watches from the window beside the walker as her car pulls
away down the street.

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. That evening she opens the front door in her scrubs, and the
evening light is warm and unmistakably different from the morning. The dog does a full spin
and a squeak and she kneels into him.

*31 spoken words. The morning-to-evening jump is carried by the light, stated in the prompt,
because a subtle time jump reads as one continuous scene.*

## Script 14 — "Pick-Up Day" (alternate to Script 13: both close on a reunion, run ONE)
**Animal:** springer spaniel, boarding kennel reunion after a week.

**0.0–3.5** She walks fast across the car park outside a boarding kennel.
> HER: "Seven days. The longest we have ever been apart, and I felt every one."

**3.5–8.0** She leans on the reception desk.
> STAFF: "You must be here for Alfie?"
> HER: "Here's his emotional support animal card."

She holds out a plain white card with a small photo of the dog and three grey lines.

**8.0–11.5** A door opens down the corridor.
> STAFF: "He never stopped watching the door."
> HER: "Alfie."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. Full-body wiggle, lead forgotten, he throws himself at her and
she goes down on her knees in the corridor to catch him. The staff member stays soft-focus
behind them.

*33 spoken words. "He never stopped watching the door" moved out of the crowded transaction
beat into the corridor beat, where it lands harder anyway.*

## Script 15 — "Half Day" (hold: runs when e1-first-day fatigues, not beside it)
**Animal:** cockapoo puppy. A natural sequel to e1: same daycare world, inverted moment.

**0.0–3.5** She arrives at the daycare front desk at noon, keys still in her hand.
> HER: "Half day today. Don't tell him."

**3.5–8.0**
> STAFF: "Early pickup. And you are?"
> HER: "His mum. Here's his emotional support animal card."

She holds out a plain white card with a small photo of the dog and three grey lines.
> STAFF: "Ah. The famous Biscuit, is it."

**8.0–11.5** In the corridor a door opens on the play room: Biscuit mid-game with one other
dog.
> HER: "Biscuit. Come on, love. Home time."

He ignores her completely.

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. Biscuit does one last gleeful lap, then gallops over and skids
into her legs. She laughs.

*31 spoken words. Deliberately shot away from e1's visual grammar: front desk and corridor,
no fence, no yard POV, one background dog only (multi-dog play scenes garble).*
