# ESA Card video scripts, for approval before anything is rendered

Nothing in this document has been generated. Standing rule from Robby, 2026-08-13:
**never render a video ad without sending the script first.**

## What was wrong with the last batch

1. **The CTA was in a human being's mouth.** Real people do not say "Emotional support
   animal card, thirty-nine dollars" to a camera. The product name and the price belong on
   an end card. Dialogue is dialogue.
2. **The story was disconnected from the product.** A man cuddling an old dog at golden hour
   is a nice fifteen seconds of film, but nothing in it involved the thing being sold, so the
   last line arrived out of nowhere.

## The three rules these scripts follow

1. **Every script is a real situation where having the card changed something.** If the card
   could be removed from the scene without changing the story, the script is cut.
2. **Nobody speaks the product name, the price, or the URL.** That is all end card. The most
   anyone says is what they would actually say in that room.
3. **The improvement is the point.** Each one has a before and an after, spoken the way a
   person would mention it, not the way an advertiser would.

The honest benefit, and the one all five are built on: **you stop having to explain your
animal to strangers.** It removes a recurring, low-grade, slightly humiliating friction. That
is what the card actually does, it is what the marketing site claims ("makes everyday moments
easier at leasing offices, with building staff, and at pet-friendly hotels"), and it is worth
$39 to a lot of people.

Every script ends on the same still end card, no voice over it:

> **Emotional support animal certificate and ID card**
> **$39, one time · esacard.com**

## Script 1 — "Thirty Seconds"
**Situation:** apartment viewings. The paperwork question used to cost her a week.

**0.0–3.0** Parked car outside a block of flats, phone selfie, staffy in the passenger seat.
> HER: "Fourth viewing this month. They always ask the same thing."

**3.0–7.5** Leasing office desk, handheld over her shoulder. The agent looks up from a form.
> AGENT: "And is he registered with anyone?"
> HER: *(already sliding a white card across the desk)* "There you go."
> AGENT: "Oh, perfect."

**7.5–11.5** Back in the car, laughing, the dog leaning over the handbrake at her.
> HER: "That used to be three emails and a week of waiting."

**11.5–15.0** She scratches his ear, looks out of the windscreen.
> HER: "Took thirty seconds."

## Script 2 — "I Used To Dread That"
**Situation:** new building, week two. The lobby conversation he had been bracing for.

**0.0–3.0** Walking into an apartment lobby, dog on a lead, handheld from behind him.
> CONCIERGE: "Sorry, mate. Is he yours?"

**3.0–7.0** He stops, pulls a card out of his wallet without really breaking stride, holds it up.
> HIM: "He is, yeah."
> CONCIERGE: *(a glance, a nod)* "Ah, no bother at all. He's a handsome thing."

**7.0–11.0** Lift doors closing on the two of them, phone held low.
> HIM: *(quietly, half to the dog)* "Two weeks ago that would have ruined my morning."

**11.5–15.0** The dog looks up at him. He laughs.
> HIM: "Now it's nothing."

## Script 3 — "Explaining"
**Situation:** the emotional one. The relief of not justifying your animal in the street.

**0.0–3.5** Park bench, low golden light, a woman in her sixties, small terrier on her lap.
> HER: "People ask. All the time."

**3.5–8.0** She keeps stroking him, not looking up.
> HER: "What is he. Why have you got him. Is he allowed in here."

**8.0–12.0** A beat. She looks down at the dog.
> HER: "I used to stand there in the street trying to explain the whole thing."

**12.0–15.0** She smiles, pats her coat pocket, stands up.
> HER: "Now I just show them, and we get on with our walk."

## Script 4 — "Section Four"
**Situation:** a rental application at the kitchen table. Funny, and honest about its limits.

**0.0–3.0** Kitchen table, laptop, a stack of forms, a large cat sitting squarely on the forms.

**3.0–7.0** He reads it out completely deadpan. The cat stares into the lens.

**7.0–11.0** He reaches past the cat, props a white card against the laptop, starts typing.
> HIM: "This used to be the bit I'd put off for a week."

**11.0–15.0** The cat lies down on the keyboard. He gives up, laughing.
> HIM: "This bit still takes a week."

## Script 5 — "The Weekend"
**Situation:** a night away that used to be a negotiation.

**0.0–3.0** Car boot open outside a small hotel, bags coming out, spaniel jumping down.
> HER: "We didn't used to bother with this."

**3.0–7.5** Reception desk. The receptionist leans over to look at the dog.
> RECEPTIONIST: "Oh, hello. Have you got his details?"
> HER: *(hands the card over)*

**7.5–11.5** Hotel room door swings open. The dog is on the bed before she is through it.
> HER: "Straight on the bed. Every single time."

**11.5–15.0** She sits down next to him and gives up on the bag.
> HER: "No phone calls. No forms. Just came."

## Notes on production

- **Two-hander dialogue is new for us.** Every previous Seedance ad was one person talking to
  a phone. Scripts 1, 2 and 5 need a second speaker and a reply, which the model can do but
  has not been asked to do on this account. If any of them renders badly, the fallback is to
  keep the scene and have the other person's line be off-camera.
- **The card stays blank** under the existing prop rule: white rectangle, small photo of the
  animal, three grey lines, nothing else. No seal, no crest, no readable text.
- **The end card is added in post**, not rendered, so the price and the URL are always
  correct and never garbled by the model.
- Cost is about **$6.94 per 15-second clip** at 720p.
