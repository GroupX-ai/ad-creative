# ESA Card | slice-of-life batch 2 | 2026-08-13

Five more 15-second clips, taking the shippable set to ten. Built to what Robby singled out
in `d1-viewing` and `d5-hotel`: authentic, wholesome, cute, and heart-tugging.

**Heartstrings was the bar these were tuned for.** The first drafts were cheerful but light,
so every final beat was rewritten to land emotionally rather than just cutely, and the prompt
carries an explicit instruction saying so: the last beat is a moment about the bond between
the person and the animal, held long enough to breathe, ending inside the moment rather than
after it.

| id | scene | animal | the beat that lands |

| `e1-first-day` | doggy daycare drop-off | golden retriever puppy | she stays at the fence, fingers hooked through the wire, not leaving |
| `e2-keys` | keys to a first flat | french bulldog puppy | he slides down the doorframe and sits on the bare floor beside him in the sun |
| `e3-back-sunday` | leaving him with a sitter | beagle puppy | she holds his face in both hands, forehead to forehead, then lets him go |

| `e5-haircut` | first ever haircut | pomeranian puppy | he trots into her arms and she buries her face in his fur |

`e2-keys` is the strongest. A man on the floor of his own empty flat with his puppy in the one
patch of sunlight, keys still in his hand.

## Settings, and where they deliberately do not go

Every setting is a private arrangement where showing an ID card is ordinary: a daycare, a
landlord, a sitter, a holiday let, a groomer. No shops, cafes, restaurants or transport
anywhere, and the ban list now names them explicitly, because emotional support animals have
no public-access rights and a scene implying otherwise is both a false claim and a policy
problem.

## QA

Seven renders for five clips. Round one returned two garbled phrases, `e1` as "emotional
**stayvie daydie** card" and `e4` as "emotional support **Aine** card". Both re-rolled clean.

Worth noting against the previous batch's finding: **putting the phrase in the owner's mouth
improves the odds but is not a guarantee.** Both failures here were owner-spoken. The reliable
control is the transcription gate, not the prompt. Every clip is transcribed before delivery
and anything garbled is re-rolled, which is now the only thing standing between a mispronounced
brand name and a live ad.

One harmless artifact left alone: `e2` ends on an unwritten "wow" during the silent beat, which
reads as a natural exhale. `e4` dropped "Here's his" and opens the line straight on the phrase,
which still plays naturally after "This is him."
