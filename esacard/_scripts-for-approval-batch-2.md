# ESA Card video scripts, batch 2, for approval before anything is rendered

Nothing here has been generated. Standing rule: **never render a video ad without sending the
script first.**

Robby picked `d1-viewing` and `d5-hotel` as the best of the first five: most authentic
feeling, animals genuinely wholesome and cute. These five are built to the same formula, which
is what those two share and the other three do not:

1. **A real errand with a transaction in it.** Somebody is going somewhere and has to hand
   something over. That is what makes the card belong in the scene instead of being mentioned.
2. **A location change with an arrival at the end.** Car to desk to room. The payoff is
   getting there.
3. **A very young, very cute animal** who is the star of every frame.
4. **A wordless final beat.** No summarising line, no pitch, no end card. It just ends.

## Two hard constraints carried over from the last batch

- **The owner says the brand phrase, never a second character.** This cost four re-rolls to
  learn. When a receptionist or a leasing agent asked for the card by name, the model returned
  "emotional support **rabbit** card", "**ham** card", "**elbow** card". Every clip where the
  owner said it came back clean. So in all five below, the other person asks a plain question
  and the owner answers with the full phrase.
- **One spoken exchange per transaction beat.** `d5` needed a fourth roll purely because three
  exchanges plus a location change inside fifteen seconds was too crowded.

## Two places these deliberately do not go

No shops, cafes, restaurants, planes or public transport. Emotional support animals have no
public-access rights, so a scene implying otherwise is a false claim and a policy problem. Every
setting below is a private arrangement where showing an ID card is an ordinary thing to do:
a daycare, a landlord, a sitter, a holiday let, a groomer.

## Script 6 — "First Day"
**Animal:** golden retriever puppy, all paws and no coordination.

**0.0–3.0** Car park outside a doggy daycare, the puppy bouncing sideways on the lead.
> HER: "First day. He's been awake since five."

**3.0–8.0** Front desk. A staff member in a fleece crouches to say hello to him first.
> STAFF: "Has he been with us before?"
> HER: "First time. Here's his emotional support animal card."
> STAFF: "Perfect. He's going to have a great day."

**8.0–11.5** Through a gate into a play yard. Three friendly dogs come straight over.
> HER: "He's not going to miss me at all, is he."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. The puppy disappears into a pile of wagging tails. She watches
through the fence, laughing, and doesn't leave.

## Script 7 — "Keys"
**Animal:** french bulldog puppy, enormous ears, permanently confused expression.

**0.0–3.5** A flat door on a landing. A building manager with a keyring. The puppy sitting
neatly, staring up at him.
> HIM: "Two months of forms for this bit."

**3.5–8.0** The manager checks a clipboard.
> MANAGER: "And he's registered with you?"
> HIM: "Here's his emotional support animal card."
> MANAGER: "Lovely. Welcome in, both of you."

**8.0–11.5** The door opens on a completely empty flat. The puppy skids in across bare boards.
> HIM: "Go on then."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. The puppy finds the one patch of sun on the floorboards and flops
into it. He leans on the doorframe watching, keys still in his hand.

## Script 8 — "Back Sunday"
**Animal:** beagle puppy, ears too big for its head.

**0.0–3.5** A doorstep, a weekend bag on the ground, the puppy already wandering inside past a
smiling sitter.
> HER: "Right. Back Sunday."

**3.5–8.0** She passes over a lead, a bag of food, and a small white card.
> SITTER: "Anything I should know?"
> HER: "Here's his emotional support animal card. He'll steal your sofa."

**8.0–11.5** Through the doorway, the puppy is already on the sofa.
> HER: "Told you."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. The puppy leans its whole weight against the sitter, who gives up
and sits down with it. She waves from the step and pretends to be offended.

## Script 9 — "The Cottage"
**Animal:** springer spaniel puppy, permanently mid-air.

**0.0–3.5** A car pulling up on gravel outside a small stone holiday cottage. The puppy at the
window, fogging it.
> HER: "Six hours in a car for this one."

**3.5–8.0** The owner of the cottage meets them at the gate with a key.
> HOST: "And this must be him."
> HER: "This is him. Here's his emotional support animal card."
> HOST: "He's very welcome."

**8.0–11.5** The gate opens onto a big open field behind the cottage.
> HER: "Go on."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. The puppy tears off across the grass at full speed, ears flying,
and immediately turns round to check she is following. She is.

## Script 10 — "First Haircut"
**Animal:** a small very fluffy pomeranian puppy, before and after.

**0.0–3.5** A grooming salon reception. The puppy on the counter looking like a dandelion.
> HER: "He has never had a haircut in his life."

**3.5–8.0** The groomer lifts him gently and looks him over.
> GROOMER: "First time with us?"
> HER: "First time anywhere. Here's his emotional support animal card."
> GROOMER: "We'll take good care of him."

**8.0–11.5** Later. The salon door opens and she stops dead.
> HER: "Oh, that is ridiculous."

**11.5–15.0** NOBODY SPEAKS. An absurdly fluffy, perfectly blow-dried puppy trots straight into
her arms. She buries her face in him.
