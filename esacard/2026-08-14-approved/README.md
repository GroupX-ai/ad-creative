## The finding worth keeping

**A multi-word brand phrase renders reliably in the main speaker's mouth and garbles in a
secondary character's line.** This cost three rounds of re-rolls to isolate, so it belongs in
the playbook.

Round 1, the phrase was a question asked by the second person in three of the five scripts.
Every one of those garbled, and the two where the main speaker said it were fine:

| clip | round 1 | who said it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| d1 | "emotional support **ABAN code**" | leasing agent |
| d2 | "emotional **sift agent** card" | main speaker |
| d3 | correct | main speaker |
| d4 | "emotional **compass card barred**" | main speaker |
| d5 | "emotional support **rabbit** card" | receptionist |

Round 2 hardened the phrase rule with per-word rhyme anchors ("emotional rhymes with
devotional", "animal has three syllables, an-i-mal") and an explicit ban on the exact
mis-hearings. That fixed **every clip where the main speaker says the line** (d2, d3, d4) and
fixed neither of the two where a secondary character asks it: d1 became "emotional support
**ham** card", d5 became "emotional support **elbow** card".

Round 3 moved the line to the main speaker in both. The other person now asks a generic
question ("And is he registered with anyone?") and the owner answers with the phrase. d1 came
back correct immediately.

`d5` needed a fourth roll on its own. Three spoken exchanges plus a location change inside
fifteen seconds was simply too crowded, and it garbled the receptionist's line too. Cutting
her line entirely, so the scene has one spoken exchange, fixed it.

Round 1 also produced a **stage-direction leak** in d4, which read "not looking up" aloud as
dialogue. The speech rule now states explicitly that anything not inside quotation marks is
stage direction and must never be spoken. No leak in any later roll.
