# 1Lookup analog + negging banner batch (2026-08-03)

Wave 2, from Robby's follow-up to the same-day disruptive batch: "Try to come up with disruptive visuals (like a writing board, etc..) Really disruptive neg titles like 'If you don't like good data, scroll on'."

Two axes, both mandatory in every concept. **The visual is an analog hand-made surface** photographed on a phone: chalk on a warehouse floor, marker on a cardboard flap, a whiteboard, sticky notes on a glass wall, printer paper taped to a coffee machine. **The headline negs the reader**: reverse psychology, anti-sell, dry and confident. Robby's own line ships verbatim on c17 and c18.

Six analog-surface lenses produced 18 concepts. The tone-and-claim gate killed 3, the portfolio pass cut 6, leaving 9. Sibling batch: `../2026-08-03-disruptive/` (12 cinematic concepts, same day).

## Why this register is claim-safe by construction

A negging headline asserts nothing. "If you don't like good data, scroll on" makes no factual claim at all, so the claim risk moves entirely to the smaller second line, where the ordinary rules apply. That makes this the safest copy register 1Lookup has tested, and it is why every second line here is a bare approved claim: `7-day free trial. No contracts.`, `Daily carrier data updates.`, `Live in 5 minutes.`

## Rules that were deliberately relaxed for this wave

1. **The 7-word headline cap was lifted to about 12 words.** A hand-written sign is allowed to be a sentence: the text *is* the image. c16 runs 8 words and c23 runs 8; both need the length to land.
2. **"Clean modern SaaS advertising design" is banned from these prompts.** It fights the concept. The prompts use photographic language instead: shot on a phone, available light, depth of field, surface texture.
3. **A rendered CTA button is optional.** On c15 and c18 the offer is carried in chalk and there is no button at all. Where a button is used it is described as composited flat onto the photograph, so it reads as an ad layer rather than something impossibly printed into the scene.

What did not relax: every factual claim still traces to the site, no accuracy percentages, no third-party brands, and the wordmark is lowercase `1lookup`.

## Killed at the tone gate, and why it matters

Negging fails in a specific way: it stops being funny and starts being smug. Three concepts were killed for it, and the reasoning is worth keeping because it is the standing test for this register.

- **`w2-paper-contract-in-red`** — "It's only money." is sarcasm aimed at a purchase the reader already signed. The object of the joke is the reader's judgment, not the problem, which is the specific failure the wave-2 tone rule exists to catch. Robby's benchmark line mocks a preference and offers an exit
- **`w2-sign-sleep-well`** — Plain self-criticism read, which the brief makes an automatic KILL. "Your numbers" spoken by a data vendor means the numbers that vendor sold you. "Sleep well. Your numbers are probably fine." signed 1lookup therefore reads as 1Lookup hedging on the accuracy of its own output: a self-inflicted accuracy disclaimer on the one attribute a validation API is bought for. This is not the acceptable ambiguity the format prices in
- **`chalk-the-lines`** — TONE, KILL-LEVEL: the concept IS the punishment-lines trope, not a roll that might accidentally read as it. A single sentence written repeatedly down a classroom blackboard has exactly one cultural meaning: a pupil being made to write lines by a teacher. The concept's own tone_check sets the kill condition ("If a roll reads as someone being made to write lines, kill it rather than ship it") and then specifies a surface that guarantees it on every roll. The defence, that the sentence is first-person plural and the room is empty, is a rationalisation the viewer never performs: the classroom slate does the framing before any word is decoded. The ad puts the reader in the chair being disciplined for their own deferral, which is scolding, and scolding cannot be fixed with a word swap.

The sharpest of those is `w2-sign-sleep-well` ("Sleep well. Your numbers are probably fine."). Spoken by a data vendor, "your numbers" means the numbers that vendor sold you, so the line reads as 1Lookup hedging on its own accuracy: a self-inflicted accuracy disclaimer on the one attribute the product is bought for. **The test to carry forward: read the negging line as if the brand signature is part of the sentence.** If it turns into self-criticism, it dies.

## Concepts, angles and watch-outs

### c15 — The Loading Dock Floor

**Surface:** chalk on sealed concrete warehouse floor, shot from directly overhead, white chalk headline with a blue chalk second line

**Copy:** "DON'T BUY OUR DATA." / "7-day free trial. No contracts."

**Why it was picked:** Ranked first because it is the hardest inversion in the batch written in the fewest words: four words that tell the buyer not to buy, resolved half a beat later by the offer line underneath. It is also the cleanest render in the set (10 hand-lettered words, all block capitals or neat print, no cursive, and overhead-on-concrete has no wipe-ghosting or shopfront signage to fight), so it is the one concept where the strongest line sits on the safest surface. PASS with no copy fixes required.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- 7-day free trial (approved claim bank)
- "No contracts" approved trust chip
- Headline makes no product claim; it is a pure anti-sell
- The only numeral anywhere in frame is the 7

**Render risk:** LOW-MODERATE, the best in the shipping set. Budget 2 to 3 rolls. Three named garble points, all cheap to police: (1) the apostrophe in DON'T, watch for DONT and DON,T; (2) the hyphen in "7-day", which models render as "7 day" or "7day" and either is an unapproved rendering of an approved number and a mandatory re-roll; (3) the hand-drawn chalk box around the wordmark, which is exactly the kind of empty container the model likes to fill, so reject any roll with any mark inside it other than the wordmark. Keep the "every stroke gone over twice" instruction, it actively buys legibility. HARD SHIPPING CONDITION carried from the audit: no crop, placement or thumbnail may run without "7-day free trial. No contracts." fully legible, because the blue line is the only thing that converts the anti-sell into an offer.

**Files:** `1lookup-c15-square.png`

### c16 — Nobody Ever Got Fired

**Surface:** cardboard box flap, fat black marker, propped on the seat of an empty office chair and taped to the chair back

**Copy:** "Nobody ever got fired for buying bad data" / "Stop paying for bad data. Cancel anytime." / button "Start For Free"

**Why it was picked:** The best-written line in the batch: it flips a real trade idiom, and the empty chair silently contradicts the sentence sitting on it, so the punchline lands about half a second after the words do. That delay is the scroll stop. Ranked second rather than first only because the fix that makes it safe (moving the wordmark off the cardboard into composited type) also costs it the fully-analog purity it was pitched on, and because the empty chair reads plausibly as "it is 6pm" rather than as irony until the composited line lands.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "Stop paying for bad data." is verbatim approved site copy (cta.tsx:75 and hero subhead)
- "Cancel anytime" approved trust chip
- CTA "Start For Free" is an approved CTA label
- Headline is an ironic industry truism and asserts nothing about the product
- No number appears anywhere in the frame

**Render risk:** MODERATE after the fix, down from high. 8 hand-lettered words on two lines with no apostrophe and no comma; the wordmark, secondary line and pill are all composited clean type, which removes what was the single riskiest character string in the whole wave-2 batch. Prompt the headline exactly as it must render, in escaped quotes, with the two-line break locked. Binding QA rule: BAD DATA is the payload of the joke, so any roll where those two words are not instantly readable is a re-roll, not a keep, and the "ran out of room" instruction must never shrink their letter height. Budget 4 to 6 rolls. Log only, not a breach: the headline is a substitution on "Nobody ever got fired for buying IBM," so a real brand is summoned by implication; no brand name appears in copy or in frame, but note it in case Meta review reads the allusion.

### c17 — The Cardboard Sign

**Surface:** torn sheet of corrugated cardboard, thick black permanent marker, held in both hands in front of a face

**Copy:** "If you don't like good data, scroll on" / "7-day free trial. No contracts." / button "Start For Free"

**Why it was picked:** Robby's own line on the one surface where a stranger physically asking you to stop is the native grammar, and the hidden face turns it into a message rather than a person, so the eye reads the words before it classifies the frame as advertising. It is also the safest execution of that line anywhere in the batch: only the headline is hand-lettered and everything else is clean composited type. Chosen over the whiteboard, sticky, notebook-page and taped-dashboard versions of the same headline because it renders cleanest and is the only one whose composition is genuinely tall.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "7-day free trial" from the approved numbers bank
- "No contracts" approved trust chip
- CTA "Start For Free" is an approved CTA label
- Headline makes no factual claim of any kind; it is a dismissal

**Render risk:** LOWEST hand-lettering exposure in the shipping set, but not free. 8 hand-lettered words on four lines at 2/2/2/2 words, carrying the only two punctuation marks in the concept: one apostrophe and one comma. Prompt the string exactly as it must render, in escaped all-caps quotes with the four line breaks locked, because the headline field is sentence case and the render is capitals, and that mismatch invites the model to guess. Read every roll letter by letter at the apostrophe. Approved fallback if the comma will not hold across three consecutive rolls: drop it, the line break after DATA already supplies the pause. ### c18 — The Sidewalk Board

**Surface:** cafe A-frame sandwich board on a sidewalk, black slate panel, white and blue chalk markers

**Copy:** "IF YOU DON'T LIKE GOOD DATA, SCROLL ON" / "Carrier data, updated daily."

**Why it was picked:** The strongest medium-message mismatch in the batch: a physical street sign telling you to scroll only makes sense because it is sitting in a feed, and that collision registers before the frame is classified as an ad. It is the second and last outing for Robby's line, deliberately on a surface as far from the cardboard sign as the set allows (outdoors, chalk, commercial, wide), and it is the only genuinely landscape-native surface in the shipping set. Ranked below the cardboard sign purely on render cost.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "Daily" FCC & Carrier Data Updates, rendered as "Carrier data, updated daily." with the US-only regulator dropped, the same treatment already cleared on wave-1 c9
- Headline makes no factual claim at all; it is Robby's verbatim reverse-psychology line
- No numeral appears anywhere in the frame, so there is nothing to over-claim

**Render risk:** MODERATE-HIGH. 13 hand-lettered words (8 headline + 4 secondary + the wordmark), just over the ~12-word ceiling, in three lines of chalk-marker block capitals. Budget 4 to 6 rolls, not 2, and read every roll letter by letter at full size. Three named failure points: (1) the apostrophe in DON'T, which models drop, double or convert to a comma, producing DONT or DON,T; (2) the three-line break, where models pad short lines with invented filler words, so lock the breaks in the prompt exactly as written; (3) the wipe-ghosting, which is the single likeliest failure, since the original instruction contradicted itself by asking for a wiped menu and no other writing at once. The visual above resolves it to smeared tonal haze with no letterforms, and any roll with a legible ghost word or price is a mandatory re-roll. Do NOT shorten the secondary to buy render odds: "updated daily" is what makes the claim traceable and it matches the treatment already cleared on wave-1 c9. Also reject any roll with legible background shopfront lettering.

### c19 — The Wall

**Surface:** glass conference wall covered in yellow sticky notes, one brand-blue note in black Sharpie

**Copy:** "Keep doing it by hand." / "Live in 5 minutes." / button "Start For Free"

**Why it was picked:** The strongest single photograph in the set: a wall of somebody's sticky notes is a picture of real labour, and nobody in growth or ops can look at it without recognising their own process, so the blue note answering the yellow wall carries the whole argument with no diagram and no product shot. The fixed headline is also what makes it shippable: "Keep doing it by hand." grants permission instead of grading effort, which removes the parent-to-child register the original line had and de-links the tone from whether the wall happens to render impressively. Ranked here rather than higher because the ~17-note squiggle field is the largest garble surface in the batch.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "Live in 5 minutes" from "Live in 5 minutes or less" (cta.tsx:28) and "Start free in 5 minutes."
- CTA "Start For Free" is an approved CTA label
- Headline makes no factual claim; it grants permission to keep doing the manual version
- Yellow notes carry no legible text at all, so no stat can be invented on them

**Render risk:** HIGH on the wall, LOW on the lettering. Only 5 hand-lettered words on the blue note with no apostrophe, the safest lettered copy in the shipping set. The exposure is the sticky field: image models form letter-like and digit-like shapes on repeated paper surfaces by default, and a single note rendering a readable word or number is an invented stat and a live guardrail breach. Three mitigations, all applied above: note count cut from ~40 to 16-18, everything past the front row pushed into heavy defocus, and an explicit instruction that every yellow note bears only continuous wavy squiggle with no letterforms, digits or punctuation. Binding QA rule: inspect at 100 percent and read every resolvable note individually before shipping, not just the sharp blue one. Prompt hygiene: "yellow sticky note" throughout, never the 3M trademark. Budget 3 to 5 rolls.

### c20 — Don't Believe This Whiteboard

**Surface:** office whiteboard, black dry-erase marker headline with a blue marker second line, blue marker held in frame

**Copy:** "Don't believe this whiteboard." / "Check the data yourself. 7-day free trial."

**Why it was picked:** The only concept in the set that jokes about its own surface, which is what makes it read as written rather than bought: an ad that opens by telling you not to believe it disarms the reflex to discount it, then resolves in the same glance into "so go check it free for a week." It also lands on the surface Robby named first ("a writing board"), and it is the lowest-render-risk whiteboard of the three candidates. Ranked mid-pack because the line is a format joke rather than a business one, so it will likely win attention and lose intent against the top three.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "7-day free trial" is an approved claim from the bank
- "Check the data yourself" promises a test, not an accuracy number, which is the only accuracy-adjacent framing the site allows
- Headline makes no factual claim; it is an anti-sell instruction
- The only numeral in frame is the 7 in "7-day"

**Render risk:** LOW-MODERATE, and the fix keeps it that way. 11 hand-lettered words (4 headline, 7 second line), inside the ~12-word ceiling, with the wordmark composited rather than drawn. The fix is compositional, not verbal: the hand-drawn arrow and the blue box are deleted, because as originally specced the only directional element in the frame aimed the disbelief instruction straight at 1Lookup's own offer terms. Reject any roll that reintroduces an arrow, box, circle or underline. Three garble points to read letter by letter: the apostrophe in DON'T, the terminal period after WHITEBOARD, and the hyphen-plus-digit in "7-DAY", which is the most likely thing to smear into "7 DAY" or "7-DAYS"; a misrendered trial length is an unapproved number in a live ad, so treat any deviation as a mandatory re-roll. WHITEBOARD is the longest single string at ten letters and the most likely word to lose or double a letter. HARD SHIPPING CONDITION: the second line must survive every crop, because without it the ad is only a joke and says nothing. Budget 2 to 3 rolls.

### c22 — Blank Fields Build Character

**Surface:** laser-printed sheet in plain office sans taped over the wired-glass window of a server room door

**Copy:** "BLANK FIELDS BUILD CHARACTER." / "or you could just enrich any contact."

**Why it was picked:** It reads as a real passive-aggressive printed office notice, the genre people actually stop and read to the end, and the mock-motivational line lands as a joke before it lands as an ad. It is also the safest concept in the set on every axis: the headline never uses the second person, so it mocks a condition rather than a reader, and it is the only concept with no hand-lettering anywhere. Ranked eighth because it is the weakest stopper: blank fields are a CRM problem and a server room door is infrastructure, so the joke has to travel further than any other in the set.

**Claims used, all traceable to the site:**
- "Enrich Any Contact." is the verbatim hero H1 second line (hero.tsx:694)
- Headline makes no factual claim; it is a mock-motivational joke about empty CRM fields
- No numbers, no CTA, no accuracy language anywhere

**Render risk:** LOWEST in the batch by a wide margin. 11 laser-printed words, plain office sans, no script, no apostrophe, no comma; printed type is where image models are strongest, so the hand-lettering ceiling does not bind here. Budget 1 to 2 rolls. The hazard is extra text, not garbled text: a server room door is the most label-prone surface in an office and the model's default is to stencil SERVER ROOM, AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY, a room number, a warning triangle or a fire placard onto the door, frame or wall, which is the single most likely reason this fails QA. Reject any roll with a character anywhere outside the sheet. Second hazard: keep the wired-glass diamond grid confined to the sheet's outer margin, because across the type it breaks letterforms and can make the lowercase l in "lookup" ambiguous, so check the wordmark at 100 percent. The subline must never be cropped, since it is what stops the server room implying the blank fields are 1Lookup's own. Option, near-free on this surface only: if this goes to a conversion ad set rather than a learning test, an approved label such as "Try For Free" set in the same office sans costs almost nothing in render risk.

## Prompts

Wave-2 prompts differ from wave 1 in three ways: photographic language throughout, the hand-lettered copy stated up front character for character (hand-lettering is where the model garbles), and no generic no-extra-text tail. Each concept carries its own tailored exclusion clause instead, because the wave-1 boilerplate names a "subheadline and button label" that several of these concepts do not have.

### `1lookup-c15-square.png`

```text
A photograph, not a designed graphic and not an advertisement layout. Shot on a phone in real available light, square framing. The hand-lettered headline must read exactly, character for character: "DON'T BUY OUR DATA.". A second, smaller line reads exactly: "7-day free trial. No contracts.". Shot from directly overhead, phone held at chest height and aimed straight down at a sealed concrete warehouse floor, the frame rotated a few degrees so the writing sits deliberately off-square. The concrete is grey and working: forklift rubber scuffs, a dried spill ring, and a faded yellow lane stripe running unfocused through the bottom of the frame. The headline is written large in thick white chalk in blocky all-capital letters across two short lines, breaking exactly as "DON'T BUY" / "OUR DATA.", every stroke gone over twice so the letters are chunky and unmistakable, loose chalk dust scattered around them and one dragged smear where a hand rested; the apostrophe in DON'T is drawn as one clean separate tick, never a loop and never a comma. Below it, in smaller neat blue chalk print on one line, sits "7-day free trial. No contracts." The brand wordmark is chalked directly under that in the same blue chalk inside a rough hand-drawn box, all lowercase as "1lookup", a blue numeral 1 then the word lookup with a LOWERCASE letter l, never a capital L; the box contains the wordmark and nothing else, no other word, digit, tick or mark inside it. Light is a single hard warm bar of late sun coming through an open loading door, cutting diagonally across the concrete and lighting the words while everything outside the bar drops into cool blue shade. A worn stub of blue chalk and the toe of the photographer's boot sit at the very edge of frame. Nothing else is written or marked on the floor: no arrows, no safety lettering, no stencilled bay numbers, no pallet labels. No other legible word, letter or number appears anywhere in the frame, and the only numeral in the image is the 7 in the blue line. Square composition. Authentic surface texture and realistic depth of field. Every letter must be clearly formed, correctly spelled and fully legible. No invented statistics, no percentages, no review scores, no third-party logos or brand names anywhere in the frame.
```
